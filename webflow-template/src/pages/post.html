Why we cook over binchotan charcoal

White oak charcoal burns hot, clean and almost smokeless — here is what it does to a skewer.

Binchotan is a white charcoal made from ubame oak, fired for weeks and then smothered with a mix of sand, earth and ash. The result is a dense, ringing stick of carbon that burns for hours at a steady, very high temperature.

Heat without smoke

Because almost all volatile compounds are gone, binchotan gives off very little smoke or flavour of its own. What you taste is the food itself — fat rendering, skin crisping, a gentle char at the edges.

“Charcoal is the one ingredient on every plate you never see.”

How we use it

We light the grill at dawn and let the coals settle before service. Skewers are turned constantly and brushed with tare only in the last minute, so the sugars caramelise without burning.

Next time you sit at the robata, watch the cooks fan the coals — that small movement is how we control the heat, zone by zone.
